v2: Substantial correction. The garden-path reading-time analysis of v1 is
withdrawn. Two errors were found: (a) the analysis sample excluded the
disambiguating word entirely, because the previous-word reading-time control was
computed after restricting to the critical region, so all ROI-0 rows were
dropped and the reported N = 95,173 comprises only the two spillover positions;
(b) within the critical region the trajectory coefficient reverses sign across
positions (spillover 1: -0.009; spillover 2: +0.010; interaction p = 1e-8), so
the pooled estimate does not describe a consistent effect. The model-scale
analysis (v1 Table 5) was computed on the same sample and is also withdrawn.
The v1 orthogonality claim is withdrawn: on the verified pipeline the
correlation between extrapolation error and surprisal is r = .31, and r = .044
was the correlation with entropy. The displacement control is corrected -- the
two measures predict in the same direction, and what survives is that
extrapolation error retains its effect when both are entered jointly while
displacement does not. The Natural Stories analysis is retained and
strengthened, recomputed on a hash-verified rebuild of the measure pipeline with
subject-level inference across 171 participants. The garden-path corpus is
retained under a different analysis: pooling across conditions and word
positions, the measure predicts reading time across all 144 sentences and 2,000
participants. Separately, the word-frequency control used in v1 was computed without
normalising case or stripping attached punctuation, and was zero for 19.7% of
tokens; every frequency-dependent analysis has been recomputed with a corrected
control, which reduces the headline improvement in fit from dAIC 112 to 78.4
without changing any conclusion. New analyses add surprisal controls drawn from
larger models (GPT-2 Medium/XL, Pythia-410M). Conclusions are narrowed to
self-paced reading.
This version also reframes the contribution around a corpus-level property
measured here: the direction fitted to preceding words predicts the next
representational step at 0.44 against a 0.03 baseline, decaying within a word,
so the state sequence is not first-order Markov. A new preregistered analysis
shows neither this structure nor the reading-time effect reduces to constituent
structure: directional coherence is flat across constituent boundary depth
(syntax absorbs 3% of its variance), and the reading-time effect is strongest
within phrases (79.5% of participants) rather than at boundaries (61.0%),
inverting the wrap-up prediction. Preregistration, one documented deviation,
and all outputs are in the repository.
